# Phase 1 — Business Case: NorthStar Retail UK

> **Simulated project.** NorthStar Retail UK is a fictional company. All data is synthetic and
> generated by `scripts/01_generate_data.py`. Nothing here describes a real client, employer or
> financial result.

## 1. Executive scenario

NorthStar Retail UK is a mid-sized general-merchandise retailer selling clothing, footwear,
home, electronics, beauty, toys, sports and accessories. It trades through **24 physical stores**
across the UK and an **e-commerce operation** (website, mobile app and click & collect).

Every Monday the leadership team receives four separate reports:

| Report | Owner | Problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Store sales spreadsheet | Retail Operations | Gross sales only, no cost or margin |
| E-commerce dashboard | Digital team | Uses "orders shipped", not "orders placed" |
| Finance P&L extract | Finance | Monthly, arrives 10 days after month end |
| Returns log | Customer Service | Not linked to the original sale |

The figures disagree. When the CEO asks *"Did we make more money this quarter, and where?"*, three
people give three different answers.

## 2. Problem statement

> NorthStar's revenue is growing, but leadership cannot see **where profit is being made or lost**.
> Some categories have weak margins, some stores and regions underperform, discounting may be
> eroding profitability and returns are rising. There is no single, trusted source for KPIs, so
> decisions are slow and based on conflicting numbers.

## 3. Business objectives (SMART)

| # | Objective | Measure of success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O1 | One trusted source of truth for sales, profit and returns KPIs | All dashboard KPIs reconcile to the cleaned database within 0.1% |
| O2 | Show where profit is made or lost, by category, product, store, region and channel | Users can drill from company level down to individual transactions |
| O3 | Quantify how discounting affects margin | Margin reported by discount band; break-even discount level identified |
| O4 | Explain the rise in returns | Return rate by category, channel and reason, with trend |
| O5 | Understand customer value | New vs returning customers, segment value, repeat-purchase rate |
| O6 | Recommend actions | At least 8 prioritised recommendations with stated assumptions |

## 4. Scope

**In scope**
- Sales transactions for Jan 2024 – Dec 2025 (24 months), all stores and online channels
- Returns linked to their original sales line
- Product, customer, store, region, channel and date dimensions
- Data-quality assessment and documented cleaning
- PostgreSQL database and 30+ business queries
- Power BI semantic model, DAX measures and a 5-page dashboard
- Insight report, recommendations and executive presentation

**Out of scope** (and why)
- **Inventory and stock levels.** No reliable stock data exists yet; listed as a next phase.
- **Marketing spend and ROI.** Campaign cost data is not available.
- **Staff costs, rent and overheads.** The analysis covers *gross* profit (revenue minus cost of
  goods), not net profit.
- **Real-time reporting.** A daily refresh is enough for this audience.
- **Forecasting and machine learning.** Descriptive and diagnostic analysis comes first.

## 6. Key business questions

1. What is happening? Revenue, profit, margin, orders, AOV and their trends.
2. Why is it happening? Category mix, discounts, channel shift, returns.
3. Where is it happening? Which regions, stores, products and customers.
4. What is the business impact? Profit lost to discounts and returns.
5. What should management do? Prioritised, costed recommendations.

## 7. KPI definitions (agreed upfront so every report means the same thing)

| KPI | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Gross Sales | Quantity × list unit price |
| Discount | Gross Sales × discount % |
| **Revenue (Net Sales)** | Gross Sales − Discount (excl. VAT) |
| Cost (COGS) | Quantity × unit cost |
| **Gross Profit** | Revenue − Cost |
| **Gross Margin %** | Gross Profit ÷ Revenue |
| Orders | Count of distinct OrderID |
| Customers | Count of distinct *known* customers (guest in-store sales excluded) |
| **AOV** | Revenue ÷ Orders |
| **Return Rate** | Refund value ÷ Revenue (value basis); a units basis is also shown |
| Net Revenue after Returns | Revenue − Refunds |
| YoY % | (This period − same period last year) ÷ same period last year |

## 8. Assumptions

- Prices are excluding VAT. Revenue is recognised on the order date.
- A return is refunded at the price paid after discount.
- Returned items are **not** resold; refunds reduce revenue, but cost is not recovered (conservative).
- In-store shoppers who don't scan a loyalty card are recorded as *Guest* (unknown customer).
- One online "store" (E-commerce Hub) holds all web, app and click & collect orders' fulfilment.

## 9. Constraints

- Source systems can't be changed, so cleaning must happen in the pipeline.
- No budget for new tools beyond Excel, PostgreSQL and Power BI.
- Data arrives as CSV extracts with known quality problems.
- Analysis must stay explainable to non-technical managers.

## 10. Success criteria

- Leadership uses **one** dashboard in the weekly meeting instead of four reports.
- KPIs reconcile between SQL and Power BI.
- Every major insight follows *Finding → Evidence → Cause → Impact → Recommendation → Outcome*.
- Data-quality issues are documented and measured, not silently fixed.
