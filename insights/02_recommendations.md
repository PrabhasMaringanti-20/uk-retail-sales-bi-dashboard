## Benefit calculations (assumptions written out)

**R1 Discount guardrails (Electronics).**
2025 Electronics lines at ≥20% discount: revenue £123.9K, gross profit **−£24.7K**, discount £56.3K.
→ Gross sales £180.2K, cost £148.6K.
Assumptions: discount capped at 15%; 30% of those units are lost because the offer is weaker.
→ New GP = (0.85 × £180.2K − £148.6K) × 0.7 = **+£3.2K**. Benefit = £3.2K − (−£24.7K) ≈ **£28K**.

**R2 Electronics price/cost review.** 2025 Electronics revenue £879K at 11.3% margin.
Assumption: a +3 point margin from supplier terms and delisting the worst SKUs, with flat revenue → ≈ **£25K**.

**R3 Stride Lab.** 2025 revenue £217K, refunds £61.2K (28.2%). At the 2024 rate (10.2%), refunds would be £22.1K.
→ Up to **£39K**. Lower bound £20K, assuming half the returned stock can be resold or credited.

**R4 Fit guidance.** 2025 online apparel revenue £670K. Each 1 point of return rate ≈ £6.7K of refunds.
Assumption: a fit tool cuts 3 points → **≈ £20K**.

**R5 Voltix.** Refunds £13.8K at 22.0% vs a 6.1% norm → expected £3.8K → **≈ £10K**.

**R6 Newcastle.** 2025 revenue £86.1K at 16.3% discount rate, GP £30.7K. At the peer discount rate of 6.2% with
10% fewer units: GP ≈ £37.0K → **≈ £6K**.

**R7 Win-back.** 850 lapsed customers who had ≥4 orders. Assumptions: 15% reactivate, spend £300 each at 42% margin
= £16K GP, minus about £3K campaign cost → **≈ £13K**.

## Risks and how to manage them
- **Lower volume when discounts are capped:** pilot in one sale period with a control group (A/B by region).
- **Supplier pushback:** use the return-reason data from this dashboard as evidence.
- **Customer backlash to returns policy changes:** start with guidance (size notes), not penalties.

- **60 days:** size guidance on the top 50 apparel products; win-back campaign launched.
- **90 days:** measure results against the baseline in this dashboard; decide on Electronics delisting and the till capture process.
